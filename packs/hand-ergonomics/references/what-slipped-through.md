# What slipped through

Tier 3. The stories behind the pack, told without the device's numbers. Each one is
why a part of the pack exists.

---

## A print that passed every check and failed in the hand

A hand-held device was drawn, checked and printed. Every geometry check passed: the
parts were watertight, nothing clashed, the walls printed. The first person who held
it rejected it. The controls had been placed where the builder's own thumb fell, and
the field of keys reached past other thumbs. Nothing in the checks knew there was a
hand.

What the pack does about it: `ergo.digit_reach` counts the population, not the
builder. The physical claim asks for two testers chosen by their reach, one small and
one large, and never the builder by default.

## The builder's thumb was the limit

The key field was sized by what one thumb could cover. That is a sample of one. The
smaller hands of the population were never in the room.

What the pack does about it: the limiting control and the women's share are in the
verdict line. The proposer's `fit_counts.json` says how many keys of a pitch fit in
reach, so the key size is chosen against the population, not against the printer.

## The width nobody questioned

Every check passed, because no check had a width limit. The verdict came from a
person who held the print next to a device they already used every day: it was too
wide. No survey gives a width limit for a two-handed device.

What the pack does about it: nothing in a gate. A reference device the person holds
grounds cad-solid's `cad.bounding` limit, and a physical claim settles the feel
(`lenses.md`, lens 5). The proposer's `grip_spacing.json` gives the reach part of the
question: how far the grips can spread before the centre controls drop out.

## A part that stood proud of the face

A control's module stood above the face, under where the thumb travels. It passed
through an opening cut for it, so the clearance check read clear. A person saw it in
a render before any money was spent.

What the pack does about it: a lens (lens 6). A clearance check cannot tell a part
through its own opening from a part that should not be there.

## A removable item that would not go in

The device held a removable item in a clamp. The largest item it had to take fitted
when drawn in place, but not on its way in: the opening at its widest, the item tilted
along its path, hit the frame.

What the pack does about it: a pose for cad-solid's sweep (`travel_sweeps.md`, the
loading pose) and a lens here (lens 7). It is a mechanism pose, not a hand
measurement, so cad-solid's clash and clearance gates check it.

## A mechanism nobody else ships

A grip mechanism was designed from first principles before anyone looked at what two
shipped products in the same category did. Both did something simpler.

What the pack does about it: a lens (lens 8). No gate can know what the market
already learned.
